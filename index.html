Scope of this map

Includes general-purpose humanoid developers and autonomy sold into commercial or industrial operations: industrial robots, autonomous vehicles, drones and defense, construction, mining and agriculture. Coverage now includes service and household robot platforms, specialist scientific systems and physical AI development infrastructure. Dedicated appliances and purely teleoperated surgical systems are not comprehensively covered. Read the inclusion rules and limitations.

What this means for the map

Capital is an input

A financing event can fund progress, but does not establish task reliability, deployed volume or customer renewal.

Integration is a strategic choice

Figure and Waymo integrate the embodiment and autonomy. Physical Intelligence, Skild and Wayve emphasize software that can run on other hardware. Use the layer and business-model views to compare these choices.

On this page

How value becomes profit.

A useful product is not automatically a strong business. Connect the customer’s outcome to the supplier’s competitive position and the economics of delivering it.

How to use it on the map

Pick a company and a specific customer offer. Establish the task value, compare replacement options, then examine delivery costs. A layer identifies what is sold; a business model identifies how it is paid for. Neither establishes pricing power or profitability.

Example: fleet-operations software

For a hypothetical provider in C2, apply the tests to one customer offer:

  • Customer value: compare recovery time and supervision effort with and without the software.
  • Competitive position: assess the cost of migrating workflows, integrations and operating history. An API alone is not a switching cost; a sustainably lower delivery cost can also be an advantage.
  • Delivery economics: compare fees with integration, support and any human supervision the provider supplies.

The example is illustrative. Evidence must come from actual deployments, customer alternatives and company costs.

Follow the revenue after installation

Intuitive Surgical shows why sales beyond the system deserve attention. Its teleoperated surgical systems are an adjacent comparison, outside this map’s autonomy scope. The mix does not establish the same economics for autonomous robots.

Revenue category is not contract type

The Systems line includes lease revenue. The 75.4% share outside Systems is therefore not a measure of all recurring revenue. This chart also does not show margins by category.

What would weaken a value-capture thesis?

A cheaper substitute meets the same requirement

Compare task outcomes and total customer cost, including migration and revalidation. A claimed bottleneck needs evidence that substitution is difficult.

Deployment volume increases support costs as fast as fees

Track delivery cost per deployed unit or operating hour. More recurring revenue is useful only if the economics of serving it hold up.

The claimed data advantage cannot be used

Check ownership, permission, relevance and demonstrated performance gains. Capturing data is not the same as owning a useful advantage.

Use customer value, substitution and delivery costs together. Evidence of demand, revenue mix and profitability answer different questions.

Turn a market thesis into a testable question

Components & assurance

Does qualification create an expensive-to-replace dependency?

Disconfirmation: substitution with equivalent performance and little revalidation cost.
Data & model businesses

Does the provider own useful data rights or differentiated capability?

Disconfirmation: public data or open models meet the same requirements.
Operating models

Do recurring payments cover hardware, service, supervision and capital costs?

Disconfirmation: utilization or support costs erase the margin.
General-purpose platforms

Can a broader machine beat a simpler alternative at the customer’s required task?

Disconfirmation: customization and recovery costs persist across deployments.

Company-level costs, retention and intervention requirements remain the key evidence gaps. Use these questions to guide targeted company research.

Four tests of independent demand

Customer independence

Would the customer buy similar volume without an equity stake or related commercial commitment?

Commercial terms

Are supplier payments and customer orders separately disclosed and priced at market terms?

Price formation

Did an investor without a commercial relationship help set the price?

External revenue

Does the company earn repeat revenue from customers outside the strategic network?

An anchor customer’s commitment can produce useful operating experience without proving broad market demand. Undisclosed terms remain a research limitation, not evidence of misconduct.

Keep the analytical questions separate

Role: what is provided?

A dexterous hand is a component; a robot-control model is intelligence; a robot incorporating both is a system. A manufacturer building that robot is a production partner.

Market: where is it used?

Manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, defense, agriculture and other applications overlap roles. Embodiment is a separate filter. The old humanoid application tag remains only as a compatibility crosswalk for old links.

Business model: how is it paid for?

Machine sale, software licensing, component supply, RaaS and operated outcomes are commercial choices. RaaS does not create another technology layer. Chef and Cobot remain system builders; Formic is classified by its deployment-service offer.

Maturity: what has been established?

Research, pilot, commercial and scaled refer to evidence of activity. Newly discovered entries are marked Not assessed until deployment maturity is reviewed. Funding is not deployment evidence.

Development and runtime are different roles

Authored simulation and learned world models support development and evaluation. Policies that produce robot actions belong in intelligence. Dream-1 is placed with robot models because its documented offering is a world action model and robot-control API. An attractive generated video alone is insufficient for that classification.

The learning loop runs from observation to training to deployment and back to evaluation. Data rights, safety gates and real-world feedback determine whether that loop works. A technology partnership does not automatically establish data access or exclusivity.

Read a relationship before drawing a conclusion

Investor → investee, customer → provider, and owner → company have different meanings. Technology arrows retain the announcement’s recorded direction; they do not quantify dependence. Pending, blocked, ended and closed records are visibly distinct. Missing relationships indicate research gaps.

Technical crosswalk and old links

The original A / B / C technical categories remain in profiles and CSV exports, and old category URLs continue to filter the roster. They are a crosswalk, rather than the primary market structure. The five-role assignment is an author synthesis informed by NVIDIA’s train, simulate and deploy architecture, Prognos’s robotics value-chain analysis and Insight Partners’ ecosystem research. These sources inform the framework; they do not endorse every company assignment.

Separately incorporated operating companies remain distinct from products; an acquisition does not merge them into one product record.

The November 8 refresh reviewed the 91-company saved PitchBook list and the first 200 results of an all-geography Robotics and Drones screen for companies founded within the last 1,400 days, ordered by latest financing size. It favors funded and recently formed firms, so older suppliers and service providers were reviewed separately. 176 entries were added after identity and physical AI scope review. Aliases, duplicate brands, broad AI without a specific physical offering, RPA and unclear-scope stealth entries were not automatically added.

Sources appear in profiles. Subscription-only discovery is labelled explicitly; a company website link is a navigation aid and does not imply every claim on it was independently checked. New entries do not receive a commercial maturity rating from financing alone. Original profiles retain their original evidence dates.

The 100-company overview

Assignment rules

One primary functional section per entry, selected by its mapped offering. Secondary role capabilities are recorded where reviewed. Component vendors, robot OEMs, contract manufacturers, integrators and fleet operators are distinguished by the offer and responsibility they assume. A software business model does not alone determine a software role, and owning an internal autonomy stack does not make an operator a software vendor.

The complete assignment register is embedded in the artifact and retained in the source repository. It records the primary section, secondary roles, technical crosswalk, review date and assignment basis. These are editorial classifications, not externally certified company descriptions.

Financial information

Historical transaction marks retain their own source, date and review date. A separate November 8 PitchBook screen shows the latest observed financing date, type, size, post-money field and transaction status for reviewed companies. Round size is not valuation; acquisition consideration is not necessarily post-money equity value. Pending and upcoming transactions are not completed rounds. Public market caps are deliberately omitted. Do not use these snapshots as live prices.

Relationships and directional conventions

Relationship records have independent sources and review status. Checked primary records are the default. Inherited records remain available for further investigation and are clearly labelled. The row is the actor and the column the counterparty in the matrix; only company cells show type symbols. Role-pair cells show counts. Lines and arrows appear only in the atlas.

Customer records may describe pilots, announced programs or deployments. Equity does not establish procurement, exclusivity or control. Technology collaboration does not establish a measurable one-way dependency. A record’s announcement date does not prove continuous activity today. Component supply, Chinese corporate relationships and assurance providers remain undercovered.

Maturity, applications and decision tools

Research, pilot, commercial and scaled are author classifications of the mapped activity, not autonomy ratings. Not assessed is deliberately available for new discovery. Application tags can overlap; embodiment describes the mapped system form, while enabling technologies and services are labelled separately. Form assignments are author classifications from mapped descriptions; unassessed and mixed systems remain explicit. Headquarters identifies where a company is based, not where it manufactures or deploys.

Filters apply across views. CSV exports retain role, technical category, evidence and financing fields. Shortlists and private diligence notes stay in the browser’s local storage; exporting a shortlist includes those notes. A missing deployment field means not compiled, not that no deployments exist. Counts describe roster coverage, not economic importance. Organization totals deduplicate product/project records using the explicit parent registry; record totals count all mapped entries.

Sources and analytical limits

Primary product documentation and company/customer announcements support selected checks. PitchBook supports discovery and financial screen observations; its licensed descriptions and raw account research are not redistributed here. Public profile summaries are concise paraphrases. The Insights chapters contain dated market research and author hypotheses; sector definitions and periods differ from this roster.

The reliability calculator is a stated arithmetic scenario: attempted cycles × failure probability × recovery time. It is not measured uptime or a production simulation. The map assigns no dependency, market-power or investment scores. All amounts are USD unless stated otherwise. Operating disclosure panels retain original RMB and JPY currencies and distinguish calendar quarters, calendar halves and fiscal halves. A company statement or conditional agreement is not an audited result.
